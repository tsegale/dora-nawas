Our Sacred Heritage

A Living Sanctuary in Ancient Damaraland

Nestled in the arid Aba-Huab river valley of northwestern Namibia, Doro !Nawas Conservancy is a beacon of community-based natural resource management (CBNRM). Established by the resident Damara (ǂNūkhoen) people in 1999, our conservancy spans 407,300 hectares of red sandstone mesas, vast gravel plains, and dry ephemeral riverbeds.

Unlike fenced reserves, wildlife here roams entirely unrestricted. The critically endangered desert-adapted black rhino (*Diceros bicornis bicornis*) navigates this prehistoric terrain as it has for thousands of years, tracked on foot by our proud local Community Rhino Rangers in partnership with Save the Rhino Trust (SRT).

✓ Free-Roaming Rhino Sanctuary: Largest unfenced black rhino stronghold in Africa.
✓ Community Ownership: 100% of eco-tourism revenue directly benefits local residents and schools.
✓ Neighbour to UNESCO Twyfelfontein: World-renowned prehistoric San hunter-gatherer rock art.

The Epic of Rhino Roam

Tracking the Desert Titan on Foot

Black rhinos in Damaraland are remarkable: they survive on moisture-rich desert succulents such as *Euphorbia virosa* (poisonous to humans and livestock) and navigate vast barren rock fields under the harsh African sun.

Through the pioneering work of Save the Rhino Trust (SRT) and Doro !Nawas Conservancy, local community trackers hold generational knowledge of the land, tracking footprints in gravel and basalt to monitor each animal's health and security.

Community Stewardship

Conservation That Empowers People

Our conservancy ensures that wildlife is recognized as a living asset rather than a liability, creating sustainable livelihood opportunities for over 1,500 community members.

Clean Water Infrastructure

Constructing predator-proof kraals and separate elephant-safe solar water reservoirs to eliminate human-wildlife conflict around village homesteads.

Youth Education & Bursaries

Conservancy dividends fund school uniforms, learning materials, and hospitality apprenticeships for the young generation of Damaraland leaders.

Equitable Benefit Sharing

Democratic Annual General Meetings (AGMs) where registered conservancy members vote transparently on budget allocations and conservation initiatives.
