Twyfelfontein (/Ui-//aes)

Bordering Doro !Nawas Conservancy lies Twyfelfontein (known in the indigenous Damara language as /Ui-//aes, meaning "jumping waterhole"). Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007, it represents one of the largest concentrations of rock petroglyphs in Africa, with over 2,500 distinct engravings carved into smooth red sandstone slabs.

Created over thousands of years by San hunter-gatherers during ritual shamanistic trance dances, these petroglyphs depict rhinos, elephants, ostriches, giraffes, and human footprint maps leading to subterranean waterholes.

Most extraordinary is the famous "Lion Man" engraving, portraying a lion with five toes and an upturned tail ending in a pugmark, symbolising the supernatural transformation of shamans into powerful predators.

The Damara (ǂNūkhoen) People

The resident guardians of Doro !Nawas Conservancy belong to the Damara ethnic group, one of the earliest human populations to inhabit Namibia. Speaking Khoekhoegowab—a tonal language characterized by complex click consonants—the Damara have developed an intimate ecological wisdom of this arid country.

Historically skilled hunters, copper smelters, and pastoralists, our elders possess encyclopedic knowledge of desert plants, utilizing the bark of the shepherd's tree (*Boscia albitrunca*) and wild commiphora resins for traditional healing and spiritual ceremonies.

◆ Living Language: Preserving Khoekhoegowab click phonetics across generations.
◆ Traditional Crafts: Leather tanning, beadwork, and musical bow performance.
◆ Local Tour Guides: All official Twyfelfontein site guides are local conservancy residents.
Natural Monuments

Geological Wonders Around Doro !Nawas

Damaraland is a paradise for geologists, offering tangible glimpses into the breakup of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana millions of years ago.

National Monument

The Petrified Forest

Located just east of Doro !Nawas, this site features massive fossilized tree trunks over 280 million years old, washed down ancient glacial rivers before being buried in silica-rich sand.

Volcanic Basalt

The Organ Pipes

A geological marvel situated in a narrow gorge near Twyfelfontein, showcasing perpendicular columnar dolerite pillars that resemble church organ pipes, formed 120 million years ago.

Thermal Metamorphism

Burnt Mountain

A striking, blackened ridge formed by lava intrusions that super-heated carbonaceous shale deposits, leaving behind vivid streaks of purple, charred black, and ochre rock.

Living Cultural Experience

Visit the Damara Living Museum

Experience an authentic recreation of a traditional Damara settlement. Watch master craftsmen forge tools from scrap metal, witness traditional fire-making using two wooden sticks, and listen to the haunting polyphonic songs of our community choirs.
