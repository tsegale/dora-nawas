The Namibian Model

Communal Conservation in Action

Under Namibia's pioneering 1996 Nature Conservation Amendment Act, communal rural communities were granted legal rights to manage, protect, and benefit from their local wildlife. In December 1999, Doro !Nawas Conservancy was officially registered and gazetted.

Instead of centralized state control, our conservancy is governed by a democratically elected Management Committee comprising village representatives, women leaders, and traditional elders.

Every tourist permit, bed-night levy, and joint-venture lodge partnership generates direct financial returns that are reinvested into projects chosen by the community at our Annual General Meetings.

Tangible Impact

Key Community Initiatives

Explore how eco-tourism and wildlife stewardship solve real-world challenges for families living on the edge of the desert.

Coexistence

Elephant-Safe Solar Water Points

Desert elephants frequently damage rural village water pipes in search of drinking water. We construct reinforced, separate solar-pumped water dams several kilometres outside villages, providing elephants with dedicated water while safeguarding human drinking infrastructure.

Livestock Protection

Predator-Proof Kraals

To protect family goat and sheep herds from nocturnal predators such as brown hyenas, leopards, and caracals, the conservancy erects predator-proof steel mesh enclosures (kraals), drastically reducing livestock loss and preventing retaliatory killings.

Education

School Bursaries & Youth Clubs

Funding educational bursaries for local high school and university students, while supporting environmental clubs in primary schools that teach children about the ecological value of rhinos and elephants.

Employment

Hospitality & Guiding Careers

Over 85% of lodge staff at Wilderness Doro Nawas and local campsites are recruited from Doro !Nawas households, providing stable employment, professional chef and hospitality certifications, and career pathways.

Healthcare

Mobile Clinic Support

Providing dedicated 4x4 transport and fuel to assist regional nurses and medical practitioners in reaching remote desert settlements for child vaccinations, health checkups, and emergency transport.

Governance

Democratic AGMs & Audit

Conducting transparent Annual General Meetings where registered members scrutinize financial audits, debate conservation zoning policies, and vote on community dividend distribution.

Your Visit Makes a Difference

Travel That Directly Funds Community Welfare

Every visitor who pays conservancy conservation levies or joins guided excursions directly sustains these community projects. When you visit Doro !Nawas, you are an active partner in community-driven African wildlife protection.
